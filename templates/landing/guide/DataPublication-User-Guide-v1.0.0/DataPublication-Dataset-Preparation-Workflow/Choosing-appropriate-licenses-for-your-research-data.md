---
title: Choosing appropriate licenses for your research data
---
When publishing your research data, you will generally provide a usage license that stipulates the conditions for the usage of your data. Standardized **[ Creative Commons (CC)](https://creativecommons.org/about/cclicenses/)**  licenses are often utilized for digital works, as these cover most usage scenarios for copyrighted material.

CC licenses are widely used for publishing research data, but other licenses are specifically designed for data and databases (for example, licenses from the **[Open Data Commons (ODC)](https://opendatacommons.org/)** project that are most analogous to the CC licenses).

Similarly to licenses for research data and databases, there are also licenses tailored specifically for research software and code. The code you generate is an integral part of your research, and therefore its proper documentation is crucial for others to understand how you analyzed your data.

If you wish to add your software or code documentation to your dataset in DataPublication, you can consult the  [3.5.1 Choosing appropriate licenses for software and code](/DataPublication-User-Guide-v1.0.0/DataPublication-Dataset-Preparation-Workflow/Choosing-appropriate-licenses-for-your-research-data/Choosing-appropriate-licenses-for-software-and-code) section and / or get in touch with us, and we will help you further.

Regardless of what license you choose, by granting it, you **permit third parties to utilize your data or software under certain terms** that are valid as long as you actually hold the rights to the data or software.

There is often insufficient clarity on this point regarding research data, and there can be various legal implications under copyright, ancillary copyright for press publishers, data protection law, personal rights, and/or labor law.

Before licensing your data, you should conduct a legal review of the risk of **claims being asserted over the data**.

### License selection in DataPublication

Per default, the license for your dataset is automatically set to **[CC-0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/)**.

In order to choose a different Creative Commons license, please go to *Edit Dataset*, click on *Terms* and the edit mode will open.

![lic\_1](uploads/89f5ae998b93327e7ac19e4c1e065ba3/lic_1.png){width=1000 height=527}

Alternatively you can access the same edit mode by going to the *Terms* tab and clicking on the *Edit Terms Requirements* button located on the right.

![license-1](uploads/43c8ee3b4988cde060f0e4c337641474/license-1.png){width=1000 height=566}

In the edit mode, click on the desired license under *Dataset Terms* in the *License/Data Use Agreement* drop-down menu.

![license-2-edit](uploads/3cdbaa25d9a3063aeafc38e3eb10d6b5/license-2-edit.png){width=1000 height=565}

If you wish to describe any additional access information (for example regarding the place of data access or the availability status of the files) you can also do so in the same edit mode ** under *Restricted Files + Terms of Access*:

![license-3](uploads/68372ff68d9f196b7018248495496256/license-3.png){width=1000 height=637}

Please note that by default the *Request Access* option is enabled, meaning people who want to use any restricted files can request access by default. To disable it, or add *Terms of Access for Restricted Files* please follow the instructions in [3.4 Editing files (restrict access, delete, replace or edit file metadata)](/DataPublication-User-Guide-v1.0.0/DataPublication-Dataset-Preparation-Workflow/%7BEditing-files%7D).

**Remember to save the changes\!**

**Hint:** If you wish to grant a different license, please choose *Custom Dataset Terms* from the drop-down Menu. You can further specify the license in the *Terms of Use* field provided. If you wish to grant a software specific license, please consult [3.5.1 Choosing appropriate licenses for software and code](/DataPublication-User-Guide-v1.0.0/DataPublication-Dataset-Preparation-Workflow/Choosing-appropriate-licenses-for-your-research-data/Choosing-appropriate-licenses-for-software-and-code) section of this guide.

**Please note:Please note that a license, once assigned to the data, cannot be revoked\!**

**Hint:** For more information about licenses available in DataPublication, please go to the „[Choosing appropriate licenses for your research data](/DataPublication-User-Guide-v1.0.0/DataPublication-Dataset-Preparation-Workflow/Choosing-appropriate-licenses-for-your-research-data)“ section of this guide.

### In DataPublication we provide the following licenses for research data:

![](http://mirrors.creativecommons.org/presskit/buttons/88x31/svg/publicdomain.svg)As a basic rule, data not subject to protective rights (for example raw measurements from a machine) must be designated with the **“**[**Public DomainMark**](https://creativecommons.org/public-domain/pdm/)**”**. Even though you can (and should) publish such data, you do not hold any copyright to it. [More information about the Public Domain Mark](https://wiki.creativecommons.org/wiki/PDM_FAQ).

![pd](uploads/6aa1643d05efc24071a88bca6d0e238f/pd.png){width=273 height=96} If you don‘t want to place any restrictions on your data, you can pick „**[CC-zero Public Domain Dedication](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/)**“ which allows free usage. Please be aware that CC-0 is not the same as the Public Domain Mark. The Public Domain Mark means there is no copyright. In the case of CC-0 you actually waive your copyright, so the effect is like public domain.
Please note, that the unimpeded exchange and subsequent use of descriptive metadata is, if only from the aspect of findability, indispensable and an essential prerequisite for the open handling of scientific content. therefore the mandatory, optional and subject-specific metadata files upon publication in DataPublication are available to anyone under a [CC0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0) license.

![by](uploads/93d3cf83f25c793b5e35b8c379c5d590/by.png){width=269 height=93} For scientific purposes, the **[attribution license](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)** („BY“) is often a good choice, because it makes a reference to the author obligatory, which is pretty much in line with the citation principle.

**Hint:** In most cases, it is advisable to choose the CC-0 or the CC-BY licences as they are explicitly recommended by the European Union and the German Research Fundation (DFG) and because, in practice, further restrictions can often do more harm than good.

### If, due to legitimate reasons, you need to restrict the reuse of your data, DataPublication provides you also with more restrictive licenses:

![by-sa](uploads/76b50c69147175dc7e8f4dc338118f67/by-sa.png){width=268 height=93} The **[CC-BY share-alike](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/)** („SA“) license allows the distribution, modification and adaptation of the data as long as attribution is given to the creator and the modified material is licensed under identical terms (meaning also under CC-BY-SA).

![by-nc](uploads/3f4fc3fb16e0f41d29d64e7b89e0038a/by-nc.png){width=268 height=93} The [**CC-BY non-commercial**](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc/4.0/) („NC“) license allows a strictly non-commercial distribution, modification and adaptation of the data as long as credit is given to the creator.

**Hint:** As it is largely unclear/debated how the module NC is to be interpreted for research data, we recommend, if uncertain whether the CC-BY-NC license suits your dataset, to get in touch with our team.

![by-nc-sa](uploads/380542696aee322c152c0387ce312408/by-nc-sa.png){width=268 height=93} One can combine the above licenses further by requiring reusers to **[atribute the author, use the data only for non-commercial purposes and license the modified material under identical terms](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/4.0/)** (meaning also under CC-BY-NC-SA).

**Please note:** Please note, that DataPublication does not support the module non-derivative („ND“) of the CC licenses as, when it comes to research data, the eligibility of the module „ND“ is questionable. This module excludes the dissemination of modified (for example updated) research data (Based on: Baumann, P. \[2021\]. *Forschungsdatenmanagement und Recht: Datenschutz-, Urheber- und Vertragsrecht*. Feldkirch/Düns: Wolfgang Neugebauer Verlag.).

If you would like to know more about why we excluded this module, please get in touch.
